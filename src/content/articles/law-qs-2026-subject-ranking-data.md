## The Global Landscape: Where Law Programs Are Ranked

The 2026 Law subject ranking covers 403 institutions worldwide, spanning 61 countries. This is one of the broadest subject rankings in the QS portfolio, reflecting the ubiquity of legal education across national systems. The United States leads with 62 ranked institutions, followed by the United Kingdom with 43. China (Mainland) and Italy each contribute 20, while Australia follows closely with 19. Spain (15), Germany (14), Canada (13), France (12), and Brazil (11) round out the top ten countries by representation.

## The Top Tier: A Narrow Band of High Performers

The upper end of the Law ranking is exceptionally tight. Only 10 institutions fall within the top 10, and the top 20 contains 20 institutions. The top 50 includes 51 schools, and the top 100 captures 101. A median rank cannot be computed for the full 403-institution field: the institution at the median position has only a published rank range (151-200), and rank ranges cannot be used for medians.

The score distribution reinforces how compressed the top tier is. Only 7 institutions achieve an overall score of 90 or above. ## What the Numbers Mean for Prospective Students

The distribution of Law programs in the 2026 QS Subject Rankings suggests that the field is highly competitive at the top but offers substantial choice throughout the middle and lower tiers. The United States and United Kingdom dominate in sheer volume, while countries like China (Mainland), Italy, and Australia provide strong alternatives with fewer but still significant numbers of ranked programs.

The score bands indicate that 92 institutions with a published overall score score 70 or higher, which is a useful benchmark for students aiming for programs with strong overall performance. However, the 9 institutions scoring in the 60 to 69 band represent a small group, while 101 institutions with a published overall score score 60 or higher. The 302 institutions without a published overall score are listed with a rank or rank band but no overall score, and a missing overall score does not mean they scored below any threshold or were dropped from the ranking.

Because the institution at the median position has only a published rank range (151-200), no median rank can be computed for the field, which means that for students seeking a top-tier program, the competition is concentrated in a relatively small portion of the overall field.

## Data Notes

The data in this article comes from the QS World University Rankings by Subject 2026, published by Quacquarelli Symonds (QS), with a data reference date of 2026-04-03. The Law subject ranking covers 403 institutions globally across 61 countries. The ranking is based on a methodology that includes academic reputation, employer reputation, citations, H-index, and international research network scores, though this article focuses on the overall ranking positions and score bands.

The ranking was parsed by subject block and filtered by country, with ranks taken as the lower-bound integer. The country leaderboard reflects the number of institutions from each country that appear in the Law ranking, not the number of law schools in that country overall. The rank bands and score bands are derived from the published ranking data and are presented as they appear in the source.

One limitation to note: the ranking only includes institutions that meet QS's inclusion criteria, so some law schools may not appear even if they offer strong programs. Additionally, the overall score bands are based on the composite score across all indicators, and individual institutions may perform very differently on specific sub-indicators such as academic reputation versus citations. For students making school choices, this ranking should be considered alongside other factors such as curriculum, faculty expertise, and career outcomes.
