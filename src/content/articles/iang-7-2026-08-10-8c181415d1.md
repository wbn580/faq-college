## Introduction to IANG

The Immigration Arrangements for Non-local Graduates (IANG) is a visa scheme that allows non-local graduates of Hong Kong universities to stay and work in Hong Kong. There are two modes of renewal: the employment extension and the return extension. This article explains the renewal modes and the requirements for applying for permanent residence after seven years.

## Renewal Modes

Under the IANG, graduates can apply for an extension of stay under one of two modes: the employment extension or the return extension.

- **Employment Extension**: This is for graduates who have secured employment in Hong Kong. They can apply for an extension in the employment category.
- **Return Extension**: This is for graduates who have returned to Hong Kong to work. They can apply under the return category.

The specific conditions for each mode may vary, and applicants should review the official requirements before applying.

## Applying for Permanent Residence

After residing in Hong Kong continuously for seven years, IANG holders may be eligible to apply for permanent residence. The eligibility is based on continuous residence in Hong Kong for a period of not less than seven years under the IANG or other permissible residence schemes.

To apply, you must meet the requirement of continuous residence. Your absence from Hong Kong, if any, may affect your continuous residence, so it is important to keep track of your time in Hong Kong.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between employment extension and return extension?

Employment extension is for graduates who have already secured a job in Hong Kong, while return extension is for those who have previously left and are returning to work. Both allow you to extend your stay, but the qualifying conditions differ.

### Can I apply for permanent residence after seven years under IANG?

Yes, after residing in Hong Kong continuously for seven years, you can apply for permanent residence if you meet all other eligibility requirements.

### What happens if I have gaps in my residence?

Any period of absence from Hong Kong may break your continuous residence, so you should ensure your total time meets the seven-year requirement. It is advisable to consult the Immigration Department for guidance.
