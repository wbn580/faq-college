The 2026 QS World University Rankings by Subject for Engineering - Civil & Structural covers 277 institutions across 49 countries and regions, with the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) holding the number one position. ## Global Distribution: Where the Programs Are

The country leaderboard for this subject shows a clear hierarchy of institutional depth. The United States leads with 47 ranked institutions, more than double the next country. The United Kingdom follows with 27, and China (Mainland) ranks third with 24. Australia (16), Canada (12), and India (12) form a middle tier, while Italy (10), Japan (9), and Germany (9) round out the top ten countries by ranked institution count. The Republic of Korea appears with 7.

For an applicant, this distribution matters in two ways. First, a country with more ranked institutions offers more choices within a single national system, which can matter for visa, tuition, and employment considerations. Second, the spread across three continents (North America, Europe, and Asia-Pacific) means strong programs are genuinely global rather than clustered in one region. The 49 countries represented in the 277-institution list indicate that civil and structural engineering is taught at a competitive level in a wide range of national systems, not just in traditional engineering hubs.

The concentration at the very top is notable—the 90-to-99 band contains only 7 institutions, while the 80-to-89 band holds 26. Overall scores are published only for 51 of the 277 ranked institutions, all of them within the top 50, so the score bands describe that scored group and say nothing about the remaining institutions.

## What the Scores Mean for Applicants

The overall score in this ranking combines academic reputation, employer reputation, citations, and other research-related indicators. For civil and structural engineering specifically, the balance between academic and employer reputation matters because the field is heavily applied—graduates often go into infrastructure, construction, and public-sector roles where employer perception can be as important as academic standing.

The score bands indicate that 7 institutions score 90 or above, a group in a league of their own. The next group, scoring 80–89, includes 26 institutions. The 70–79 band contains 18 institutions. Notably, the published score bands stop at the 70–79 band, which holds 18 institutions. Overall scores are published only for 51 of the 277 ranked institutions; the remaining 226 are listed with a rank or rank band but no overall score, and a missing overall score does not mean the institution scored below any threshold or was dropped from the ranking.

This truncation is important context. The ranking does not include every civil engineering program worldwide; it includes those that met the criteria for inclusion in the QS subject ranking. The 277 institutions represent the top of the field globally, and the 49 countries represented are those with programs strong enough to qualify.

Compared to the overall structure of the ranking, the civil and structural engineering list shows a relatively even distribution across rank bands after the top 30. The published rank bands from 51–100 (49 institutions), 101–150 (50 institutions), and 151–200 (51 institutions) are all similar in size. This evenness contrasts with the top 30, where the bands are smaller and the scores are more tightly clustered.

The country distribution also shows some notable patterns. The United States has nearly double the number of ranked institutions as the United Kingdom, and more than triple the number of China (Mainland). However, China's 24 institutions represent a significant presence, and given the scale of infrastructure development in the region, this is likely to be a growing area of focus for applicants. Australia's 16 institutions and Canada's 12 also indicate strong national systems for this discipline.

For international applicants, the key question is often not just which country has the most programs, but which country has programs matching their specific interests. The ranking data shows that the top 10 are concentrated in a small number of countries, but the broader distribution across 49 countries means that strong programs exist in many national systems.
