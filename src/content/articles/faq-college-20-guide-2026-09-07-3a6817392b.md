## Introduction
The F-1 visa interview can feel uncertain, but knowing what to expect helps you answer clearly. This article covers 20 common questions asked by visa officers, with a framework for each. The key focus is on explaining your study plans and your intention to return home after your program.

## Questions About Your Study Plans
### 1. Why do you want to study in the United States?
Focus on the academic strengths of your chosen program. Mention specific courses, research facilities, or teaching methods that match your goals.

### 2. Which university are you going to attend?
State the name of the school clearly. Be ready to name your intended major and the degree you will pursue.

### 3. What will you study?
Describe your field of study in simple terms. Connect it to what you want to learn and why it matters to you.

### 4. How long is your program?
Provide the expected duration of your degree. Most bachelor's programs take about four years; master's programs typically one to two years.

### 5. Why did you choose this particular university?
Point to specific strengths of that school, such as faculty expertise, curriculum, or resources. Avoid generic praise.

### 6. Is your program fully funded?
Mention your funding sources, such as scholarships, family support, or savings. Be ready to explain how you will cover tuition and living costs.

### 7. What are your educational and career goals?
Outline your short-term and long-term goals. Link them directly to what you will study in the US.

## Questions About Your Return Intentions

![A student preparing for a US visa interview](https://img.faq.college/留学-大学FAQ/faq-college-20-guide-2026-09-07-3a6817392b-image-02-2026-1880x1253.jpg)

### 8. What are your plans after you graduate?
Explain your intention to return to your home country and describe the career or further studies you plan to pursue there.

### 9. How will your US education help your career in your home country?
Connect your degree to specific opportunities in your home country, such as industry needs or roles you aim to fill.

### 10. Are you planning to stay in the United States after your program?
Be honest and clear that you intend to return home. Talk about family ties, job prospects, or long-term plans that bind you to your home country.

### 11. What ties do you have to your home country?
Mention family, property, a job offer, or community involvement that demonstrates your connection.

### 12. Do you have a job waiting back home?
If you have an offer or a business, describe it. If not, explain your industry and how your US degree will help you find a role.

### 13. How will you maintain your home country ties while studying?
Talk about plans to stay in touch with family and take steps to keep your residence or visa status in your home country, such as keeping a valid passport or maintaining a tax status.

## Questions About Your Background and Intent
### 14. Why did you choose this field of study?
Explain your interest honestly. Mention experiences that sparked your interest, such as work, volunteering, or academic projects.

### 15. What is your academic background?
Summarize your previous education, noting any degrees, courses, or achievements that prepare you for the program.

### 16. What would you do if your visa is refused?
Stay calm and say you would work to improve your application or address any concerns, rather than implying you would illegally enter.

### 17. Have you ever been refused a visa?
If yes, be honest and explain what changed since. If no, simply state no.

### 18. Do you have any relatives in the United States?
Answer truthfully, but focus on your plans to return home regardless of relatives.

### 19. What are the main reasons for you to return home?
Emphasize family, cultural ties, career prospects, and long-term goals that are stronger in your home country.

### 20. Are your parents going to visit you?
Mention if they plan to, but keep the answer from implying permanent relocation.

## Conclusion
Remember, the interview is about showing you have a clear plan for education and a strong intention to return home. Be honest, concise, and specific. Use the frameworks above to practice answering these 20 common questions, and walk into your interview with confidence.
