## How the 2026 New Zealand Skilled Migrant Category Points Work

New Zealand’s Skilled Migrant Category (SMC) uses a points-based system to assess eligibility for residence. To be invited to apply, you generally need to reach a threshold of 160 points. The points are awarded for factors such as age, skilled employment, work experience, qualifications, and being in New Zealand or having a job offer.

For someone planning to study in New Zealand with the goal of migration, the key is to understand which of these factors you can influence through your choice of study and subsequent employment.

## Why Study Majors Matter for Reaching 160 Points

Your qualification is directly linked to points in the SMC. A higher-level qualification, such as a master’s degree or PhD, earns more points than a lower-level one. Additionally, studying in New Zealand itself can contribute to your points, as can having a job offer in a skilled occupation.

Some study majors are more likely to lead to skilled employment in New Zealand because they align with occupations that are in demand. Choosing a major that is directly linked to a skilled occupation—such as engineering, health, information technology, or trades—can help you secure qualifying employment more easily after graduation.

However, it is important to note that no specific major is guaranteed to give you exactly 160 points. The points system considers your entire profile, including your age, work experience, and whether you have a job offer. The same major can lead to different point totals depending on these other factors.

## How to Choose a Migration-Focused Study Plan

If your goal is to reach 160 points, consider the following steps based on how the SMC points are structured:

1. **Choose a qualification that earns maximum qualification points** – A postgraduate degree (master’s or PhD) generally earns more points than a bachelor’s degree or lower.
2. **Select a major that leads to skilled employment** – Focus on occupations that are listed as skilled in New Zealand, such as those in engineering, health, ICT, or skilled trades. This makes it easier to obtain a skilled job offer, which is a strong contributor to your points.
3. **Consider studying in New Zealand** – If you complete your study in New Zealand, you may be eligible for additional points for having a qualification from a New Zealand institution, as well as points for having studied in the country.
4. **Plan for a job offer** – A skilled job offer can significantly boost your points. Many graduates gain this through post-study work rights, so check which majors lead to occupations that are in demand and have good employment prospects.

While your choice of major is important, remember that your age and work experience also matter. Younger applicants often receive more age points, and more years of skilled work experience add more points. A balanced profile is key to reaching 160.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does studying a certain major guarantee 160 points?

No. The SMC points system evaluates multiple factors, including age, work experience, qualifications, and job offers. Your choice of major influences your qualification and employment prospects, but it does not alone determine your total score.

### Which study areas are most likely to lead to skilled employment?

Majors that align with skilled occupations in New Zealand—such as engineering, healthcare, information technology, and certain trades—tend to lead to better employment opportunities after graduation. However, this is not a guarantee, and your overall profile determines your points.

### Can studying in New Zealand give me extra points?

Yes. Under the SMC, you can earn points for having a qualification from a New Zealand institution and for having studied in New Zealand. These points can contribute toward reaching the 160 threshold.

### Should I choose a higher-level qualification to get more points?

Generally, yes. Higher-level qualifications (such as master’s degrees) earn more qualification points than lower-level ones. This can help you reach 160, but you still need to meet other criteria, such as skilled employment.

### Is it better to have a job offer before applying?

Having a skilled job offer can add significant points to your application. Many graduates secure employment through post-study work rights, which can make reaching 160 more achievable.
