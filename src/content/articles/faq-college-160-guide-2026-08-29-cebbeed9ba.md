## Understanding the 2026 Points System

The 2026 New Zealand Skilled Migration (SMC) points system awards points for a range of factors. A key aim for many applicants is reaching the 160-point threshold, which is the minimum required to be invited to apply for residence. Points are granted for qualifications, skilled employment, and other attributes. Your study major can influence the points you receive, particularly through the qualification level and whether it aligns with an occupation in demand.

## Majors That Help Reach 160 Points

Certain study majors are linked to higher point values. For example, qualifications at Level 9 or above, such as a master's degree, earn more points for qualification level than a bachelor's degree. Additionally, if your major leads to an occupation that is listed as skilled and there is a shortage or high demand, you may qualify for bonus points. Fields such as engineering, ICT, health, and construction-related professions often fall into this category. However, the points system considers the specific qualification and occupation, not just the major itself.

## Choosing a Study Major with Migration in Mind

When selecting a major, consider its level and how it maps to an occupation that receives skilled employment points. For instance, a master's in engineering can give you qualification points plus an extra bonus for having a master's degree. Also, if you secure a job in that field after graduation, you earn points for skilled employment. The combination of your qualification level, occupation, and job offer can help you reach 160 points. It is important to research the current skilled occupation list and the points for each category.

## Key Factors Beyond Your Major

Your major is not the only factor. Points are also awarded for age, work experience, and whether you have a job offer in New Zealand. To reach 160, you need a strong overall profile. For example, a younger applicant with a master's degree and three years of work experience might score well even without bonus points. The system rewards a balanced combination of factors, so focus on both your study choice and your career plans.

## Summary of Points to Consider

- Qualification level: Higher levels (e.g., master's) earn more points.
- Occupation: Majors leading to skilled occupations may receive extra points.
- Job offer: Having a skilled job offer adds points.
- Other factors: Age, work experience, and investment also contribute.

Ultimately, the 2026 points system requires a strategic approach. By choosing a major that is both high-level and in demand, you increase your chances of reaching the 160-point threshold. Keep in mind that the points system is subject to change, so always verify the latest information from official sources.
