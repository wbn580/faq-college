## Is it a real bachelor degree?

Yes. The three Applied Degrees — Bachelor of Applied Business (Business Management), Bachelor of Information Technology (Cyber Defence and Networking), and Bachelor of Information Technology (Software Development) — are Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) level 7 bachelor degrees. Each runs for three years of full-time study, worth 144 units of credit in total, delivered across six semesters.

The degrees are awarded by UNSW College, an institute of higher education owned and backed by UNSW Sydney. That distinction matters. UNSW College is a separate registered provider, not a faculty of UNSW, so the qualification is a UNSW College bachelor degree, not a UNSW Sydney degree. What the backing means in practice: UNSW owns and stands behind the college, and students study on the Kensington campus with access to UNSW facilities, clubs and societies.

## Is it a UNSW degree?

No. The award is issued by UNSW College, not by UNSW Sydney. The two are not equivalent qualifications, and you should not treat them as interchangeable.

What you can rely on is the ownership structure. UNSW College is owned and backed by UNSW, and its students study on campus with access to UNSW facilities, clubs and societies. If a UNSW-branded degree is a hard requirement for you, this is not that product — but if you want a bachelor qualification from a college with UNSW's backing, a smaller class structure and a project-based teaching model, it is worth looking at seriously.

## Are there exams?

No traditional final exams. Assessment across all three degrees is project-based and applied. You are assessed on work you produce — reports, builds, presentations, group projects — rather than sitting end-of-term exam papers.

Class sizes sit at roughly 20 students, compared with the 25–35 typical of a traditional degree. The teaching model is built around small-group, project-driven work, with industry-informed projects embedded through the degree.

## How long is it?

Three years, structured as six semesters across 12 terms. Each term runs eight weeks, and you take two courses per term — eight courses per year. The full degree is 144 units of credit, at 48 per year.

The three-year structure breaks down as: Year 1 at diploma level, Year 2 at associate degree level, and Year 3 at bachelor level. That is how the course is registered and sequenced, not a promise that you can leave early with a lesser award. Nothing in the official material commits to issuing a diploma or associate degree if you exit partway through, so plan for the full three years.

## What does it cost?

Tuition for 2026 is A$34,080 per year, putting the three-year tuition total at A$102,240. The CRICOS register additionally records A$1,000 of non-tuition fees, for an estimated total cost of A$103,240.

Two things to note. First, these are 2026 figures — the provider states 2027 tuition is still to be updated, and fees are reviewed annually. Second, the total assumes typical full-time enrolment and can vary depending on the courses you choose.

## When does it start?

The first international intake is Term 1, 2027, with classes starting 1 March 2027. There is also a Term 4 intake with classes starting 18 October 2027. New students commence in Terms 1 and 4.

If you need to improve your English first, there is an online pathway. The UEEC10 English course runs online from home, and the windows are timed to leave a gap before classes begin. For the March 2027 intake, the course runs 12 October to 18 December 2026, finishing in time for orientation on 25 February 2027. For the October 2027 intake, there are two options: 24 May to 30 July, or 28 June to 3 September, both finishing ahead of the 18 October class start. The provider lists applying for a visa and booking accommodation and flights in that gap — but visa requirements themselves are something to check with official sources, not something this article can advise on.

## What English score do I need?

You must meet both the overall score and the individual component requirements where they apply. The table below shows the three main tests.

| Test | Overall | Writing | Reading | Listening | Speaking |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| IELTS | 6.5 | 6.0 | 6.0 | 6.0 | 6.0 |
| PTE Academic | 64 | 54 | 54 | 54 | 54 |
| TOEFL iBT | 90 | 23 | 13 | 12 | 18 |

There are also alternatives: a C+ in Go8 Foundation English, or 70% in a Non-Go8 Australian Foundation Studies Program. If your qualification is from outside Australia and you have not sat one of these tests, a formal English test is required.

## Is there a placement?

Yes. Every Applied Degree includes an Industry Project / Industry Placement worth 12 units of credit, taken in Year 2 or Year 3 at your choice. It involves up to 160 hours of experience — 20 hours per week across eight weeks — and can be delivered through self-sourced placements, UNSW College-facilitated opportunities, work-based experiences, or industry and project-based alternatives.

There is also an eight-week Capstone Project in the final year, in Business or IT depending on your degree. For the two IT degrees, the industry alignment includes AWS Academy, Microsoft Learn for Educators, CompTIA, and (ISC)², and eligible students receive one funded certification exam per relevant course completion.

## What happens after graduation?

You graduate with an AQF level 7 bachelor degree awarded by UNSW College. The career directions the provider lists are specific to each degree:

- **Business Management**: business and operations management, project management and team leadership, business analysis and business intelligence, HR and people-focused roles.
- **Cyber Defence and Networking**: cyber security or cyber defence analyst, network administrator, SOC analyst, penetration testing, security consulting or cloud security.
- **Software Development**: software developer, AI and machine learning, full-stack or application developer, DevOps, cloud application or AI solutions roles.

The provider's launch presentation cites a forecast of up to 400,000 additional IT workers needed in Australia by 2030, and a figure that only 42% of employers believe business graduates have sufficient workplace skills. Those are the provider's own cited figures, not independent research or government data, so weigh them accordingly.

What this article will not do is promise you a job, a salary, a visa outcome, or migration benefits. None of those are verified, and any source that guarantees them for this degree is overstating.

## Scholarships

Two scholarships are available on the launch presentation's authority.

The **Applied Leadership Award** is worth up to A$10,000 per year, awarded on merit to a limited number of students. You apply with a 500-word personal statement on your motivation for studying a UNSW College Applied Degree, plus a resume or CV.

The **UNSW College Family Discount** offers up to a 10% tuition discount for a student with a relative who graduated from UNSW College or is currently enrolled in Foundation Studies or a Diploma there. Applied degree students can apply for it. The public family discount page has not yet been updated to cover the Applied Degrees, so confirm current conditions with the provider — but the discount does apply.
