## Is it a real bachelor degree?

Yes. The three Applied Degrees at UNSW College are Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) level 7 bachelor degrees, the same level as any Australian undergraduate degree. They are awarded by UNSW College, which is an institute of higher education owned and backed by UNSW Sydney. That distinction matters: the degree is a UNSW College award, not a UNSW Sydney degree. What the backing means in practice is that UNSW owns the college, and students study on the Kensington campus with access to UNSW facilities, clubs and societies.

The three programs are:

| Program | CRICOS code | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Bachelor of Applied Business (Business Management) | 120245C | 3 years (156 weeks) |
| Bachelor of Information Technology (Cyber Defence and Networking) | 120253C | 3 years (156 weeks) |
| Bachelor of Information Technology (Software Development) | 120244D | 3 years (156 weeks) |

One small correction worth noting: the launch presentation for the Software Development stream printed CRICOS code 120243E, but that code belongs to the Associate Degree of Information Technology (Software Development). The bachelor's correct code is 120244D, which is what the CRICOS register records.

## Is it a UNSW degree?

No. The award is issued by UNSW College, not by UNSW Sydney. The two are related — UNSW owns and backs the college — but the qualification is not a University of New South Wales bachelor degree, and you should not treat it as one. If the distinction matters for your plans, check with the provider directly before applying.

## How is it structured?

Each degree is 144 units of credit (UOC) over six semesters, delivered in 12 eight-week terms — two terms per semester. You take two courses per term, eight courses per year. Class sizes are around 20 students, compared with the 25–35 typical of a traditional degree.

The three-year structure maps to three levels: the first year covers diploma-level material, the second year associate-degree-level material, and the third year bachelor-level material. That is how the provider describes the progression. Nothing in the published material promises that leaving early earns you a diploma or associate degree, so do not assume an early exit produces a lesser award.

## Are there exams?

No traditional final exams. Assessment is project-based and applied — you are graded on projects, practical work and industry-informed tasks rather than sit-down exam papers. The provider describes the assessment style as project-based applied assessment with no traditional final exams.

![Students collaborating around a whiteboard on a group project, illustrating the project-based assessment style used across the Applied Degrees](https://img.ulec.com.cn/edu/unswc-applied-2027-project-based-learning-2026-1880x1253.jpg)

## Is there a work placement?

Yes. Each degree includes an Industry Project / Industry Placement component worth 12 UOC, which you can take in Year 2 or Year 3. The CRICOS register records the work component as 20 hours per week over 8 weeks, totalling 160 hours. The provider's deck describes it as work-integrated learning "integrated into the degree" including "up to 160 hours of experience."

The live course pages list four delivery options: self-sourced placements, UNSW College-facilitated opportunities, work-based experiences, and industry or project-based alternatives. There is also an 8-week Capstone Project in the final year — a Business capstone for the business degree and an IT capstone for the two IT degrees.

## What does it cost?

Tuition for 2026 is A$34,080 per year, which comes to A$102,240 for the three-year degree. The CRICOS register additionally records A$1,000 in non-tuition fees, for an estimated total of A$103,240. Two caveats: the 2026 figure is the published one, and the provider states 2027 tuition is still to be updated. Fees are reviewed annually and may change.

## When does it start?

The first international intake is Term 1, 2027, with classes starting 1 March 2027. There is a second intake in Term 4, 2027, with classes starting 18 October 2027. New students commence in Terms 1 and 4.

| Term | Week one starts | Census date | Term ends |
|---|---|---|---|
| Term 1, 2027 | 1 March 2027 | 19 March 2027 | 23 April 2027 |
| Term 2, 2027 | 17 May 2027 | 4 June 2027 | 9 July 2027 |
| Term 3, 2027 | 2 August 2027 | 20 August 2027 | 24 September 2027 |
| Term 4, 2027 | 18 October 2027 | 5 November 2027 | 10 December 2027 |

Orientation for Term 1, 2027 is 25 February 2027, and for Term 4, 2027 it is 15 October 2027. (The launch presentation gives 14 October on one slide and 15 October on its key-dates slide; the key-dates figure is used here.)

If you need to meet the English requirement first, there is an online English pathway (UEEC10) you can complete from home. One package runs 12 October to 18 December 2026, leading to the February 2027 orientation and the 1 March class start. Two further packages run May–July and June–September 2027, both leading to the 18 October 2027 start. The provider notes that the gap between the English course and classes leaves time to apply for a visa and book accommodation and flights. That is the provider's own guidance — visa requirements themselves are not covered here.

## What do I need to get in?

## What English score do I need?

You must meet both the overall and the component scores where applicable.

| Test | Overall | Writing | Other components |
|---|---|---|---|
| IELTS | 6.5 | 6.0 | 6.0 |
| PTE Academic | 64 | 54 | 54 |
| TOEFL iBT | 90 | 23 | Reading 13, Listening 12, Speaking 18 |

Two alternative English qualifications are accepted: a C+ in Go8 Foundation English, or 70% in a Non-Go8 Australian Foundation Studies Program.

## Are there scholarships?

The Applied Leadership Award offers up to A$10,000 per year, awarded on merit with a limited number available. It requires a 500-word personal statement on your motivation for studying a UNSW College Applied Degree, plus a resume or CV. One important caveat: this award appears only in the provider's August 2026 launch presentation and has no page of its own on the UNSW College website. Confirm the details with the provider before relying on it.

The UNSW College Family Discount needs careful reading because the sources disagree. The launch presentation lists it on the Applied Degrees scholarship slide. But the official Family Discount page states the 10% discount applies to Foundation Studies or Diploma programs, explicitly excludes the Diploma of Business and the Commerce Foundation streams, and is valid only for students commencing in the 2026 intake. That page does not mention Applied Degrees. So: do not assume an Applied Degree student qualifies. Check the scope with the provider.

## What happens after graduation?

The degrees are designed to produce graduates ready for specific roles. The business stream points toward business and operations management, project management, business analysis, and human resources roles. The cyber stream points toward cyber security analysis, network administration, Security Operations Centre (SOC) analysis, and penetration testing or cloud security. The software stream points toward software development, AI and machine learning, full-stack or application development, and DevOps or cloud application roles.

The provider's launch presentation cites two market figures: forecast demand for up to 400,000 additional IT workers in Australia by 2030, and a claim that only 42% of employers believe business graduates have sufficient workplace skills. These come from the provider's own presentation and carry no cited original source — treat them as the provider's claims, not independent research or government data.

What the published material does not cover: employment outcomes, salaries, visa outcomes, migration or permanent-residency benefits, and whether the qualification can be certified by authorities in your home country. None of these are verified in the source material, so they are not addressed here. If any of these determine your decision, raise them directly with UNSW College.
