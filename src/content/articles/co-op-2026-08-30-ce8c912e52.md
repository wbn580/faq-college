## What Are Co-op Programs?

Co-op programs combine academic study with paid work terms related to your field. At Canadian universities, these programs let you gain hands-on experience while earning money and earning academic credit.

## Top Universities with Co-op Programs

Several Canadian universities are known for their co-op offerings:

![University campus where co-op programs are offered](https://img.ulec.com.cn/留学-大学FAQ/co-op-2026-08-30-ce8c912e52-image-02-2026-1880x1253.jpg)


- **University of Waterloo**: Often considered the largest co-op program in the world, with over 20,000 students in co-op across more than 100 programs.
- **University of British Columbia (UBC)**: Offers co-op in arts, science, engineering, and commerce, with over 4,500 students participating each year.
- **University of Toronto**: Provides co-op options in computer science, engineering, and management.
- **University of Alberta**: Has co-op in many undergraduate programs, including engineering and business.
- **Simon Fraser University**: Offers co-op in over 50 programs, with placement rates above 90% for co-op students.

These universities integrate co-op into your degree plan, so you can graduate with significant work experience.

## How to Apply for Co-op

Applying to a co-op program usually begins before you start university. Here's the typical process:

1. **Choose a co-op program** when you apply to the university. Some universities allow you to enter co-op directly from high school, while others require you to apply after your first year.
2. **Meet the academic requirements**: Generally, you need a minimum GPA (often around 70% or higher) and a specific course background.
3. **Submit an application** – some programs require a separate application, including a resume and personal statement.
4. **Complete an interview** with the co-op advisor or faculty to ensure you're a good fit.

Once admitted, you'll be part of the co-op program and will complete work terms in alternating semesters.

## Preparing for Co-op Interviews

Co-op interviews are similar to job interviews but tailored to the co-op program. You can prepare by:

- **Practicing common interview questions** related to your field.
- **Building a strong resume and cover letter** that highlight your skills and experiences.
- **Attending workshops or mock interviews** offered by the university's career centre.

Universities often provide resources to help you succeed, including interview practice sessions and job search strategies.

## How Co-op Helps Your Career After Graduation

Co-op provides numerous advantages for your job search:

![Student working during a co-op placement](https://img.ulec.com.cn/留学-大学FAQ/co-op-2026-08-30-ce8c912e52-image-03-2026-1733x1300.jpg)


- **Work experience**: You graduate with up to two years of relevant experience, which makes you more attractive to employers.
- **Industry connections**: You may receive job offers from your co-op employers, and many co-op students are hired full-time after graduation.
- **Higher starting salaries**: Studies show that co-op graduates often earn higher starting salaries compared to those without co-op experience.
- **Career clarity**: You can test different career paths and decide what suits you best.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do all Canadian universities have co-op programs?

No, not all. Co-op is more common at certain universities like Waterloo, UBC, and SFU. Some universities offer co-op only in specific faculties.

### Can I join a co-op program after starting university?

Many universities allow you to apply to co-op after your first year, but you need to meet certain GPA requirements and available slots. Check with your faculty's co-op advisor.

### Is co-op paid?

Yes, co-op work terms are paid. You'll receive a salary during your work terms, which helps offset tuition and living expenses.

### Does co-op extend my degree?

Typically, co-op programs take an additional year to complete because you are alternating between work and study terms. However, you graduate with both a degree and valuable work experience.
