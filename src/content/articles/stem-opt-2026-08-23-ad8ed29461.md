## STEM OPT Extension Overview

The STEM OPT extension allows eligible F-1 students with STEM degrees to extend their Optional Practical Training (OPT) period. This extension provides an additional 24 months of work authorization, for a total of up to 36 months of OPT. To qualify, you must have earned a degree in a STEM field designated by the U.S. Department of Homeland Security.

## Eligibility Requirements

To be eligible for the STEM OPT extension, you must meet several criteria:

- You must be in a valid F-1 status.
- You must have completed a bachelor's, master's, or doctoral degree in a STEM field.
- You must be currently in a period of approved OPT that is not yet expired.
- You must have a job offer from an employer that is enrolled in the E-Verify program and meets the requirements.
- You must submit the Form I-765, Application for Employment Authorization, along with required documents and fees before your current OPT expires.

## Employer Requirements

Your employer must comply with specific obligations to participate in the STEM OPT extension. The employer must:

- Be enrolled in the E-Verify program and remain in good standing.
- Provide a training plan that outlines the learning objectives, mentorship, and supervision for the student.
- Sign the Form I-983, Training Plan for STEM OPT Students, and agree to the terms.
- Report any changes in the student's employment status, such as termination or change of employer, to the Designated School Official (DSO) within a specified timeframe.
- Ensure that the student's work is directly related to their STEM degree and that they work at least 20 hours per week.

## Application Timeline

The application process should begin well before your current OPT expires. Here is a general timeline:

- Submit the Form I-765 to USCIS before your current OPT ends. It is recommended to apply around 90 days before expiration.
- Include the required fee and supporting documents, including the Form I-983 signed by your employer and your DSO.
- If your application is approved, you will receive a new EAD card with the extended end date.
- You must continue to work during the processing period while your application is pending, as long as you meet the requirements.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

To avoid losing your immigration status, be aware of these common errors:

- Failing to apply before your current OPT expires. If the application is not received by USCIS before the expiration date, you may lose eligibility.
- Not working for an E-Verify employer. Your employer must be enrolled in E-Verify, and you must verify this before accepting the job.
- Submitting an incomplete or inaccurate Form I-983. Errors in the training plan can cause delays or denials.
- Failing to report changes in employment, such as leaving the employer, to your DSO. This can lead to violations.
- Working more than 90 days of unemployment during the extension period, which can result in loss of status.

## Additional Considerations

During the STEM OPT extension, you are responsible for maintaining accurate records and complying with the terms of your training plan. If you change employers, a new Form I-983 must be submitted and approved by your DSO. Also, keep track of your total unemployment days, as exceeding the limit may affect your status.

## Conclusion

The STEM OPT extension offers valuable work experience but requires careful planning and attention to details. By understanding the eligibility, employer obligations, and deadlines, you can navigate the process successfully. Always consult with your DSO for personalized guidance, and ensure all paperwork is accurate and submitted on time.
