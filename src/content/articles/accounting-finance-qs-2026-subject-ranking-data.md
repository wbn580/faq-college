The QS World University Rankings by Subject 2026 lists 376 institutions globally in Accounting & Finance, spread across 53 countries and regions. Harvard University holds the top position. This article breaks down where these programs are concentrated, how scores cluster at the upper end, and what the rank distribution tells a prospective student choosing a school by subject.

## Global Footprint: 376 Institutions, 53 Countries

The Accounting & Finance subject ranking for 2026 covers 376 universities worldwide. That is the full global list — no country-level filtering was applied. The United States leads with 69 ranked institutions, followed by the United Kingdom with 45. China (Mainland) places third with 22, ahead of Australia (18) and France (17). Canada contributes 13, while Spain and Malaysia each have 12. Italy and Germany round out the top ten countries with 11 apiece.

The top ten countries together account for the large majority of ranked programs. For a student comparing options by country, the practical takeaway is straightforward: the US and UK offer the widest selection, but the presence of 22 Chinese institutions and 18 Australian ones signals that strong programs exist well beyond the traditional Anglo-American core. France, with 17, is the strongest continental European presence, ahead of Spain, Italy, and Germany.

## The Top 100: Where Scores Separate

Within the top 100, the score distribution reveals a steep quality gradient. Exactly 4 institutions score 90 or above. A further 20 sit in the 80–89 band, and 59 more fall between 70 and 79. That means 83 institutions score at least 70, while 101 institutions score 60 or above. These bands cover only the 101 institutions whose overall score was published; the remaining 275 institutions are still ranked but are listed without an overall score. The score bands are cumulative: 4 institutions score 90 or higher, 24 score 80 or higher, 83 score 70 or higher, and 101 score 60 or higher. All of these counts cover only the 101 institutions whose overall score was published. In other words, every institution at or above the 60-point threshold also clears every lower threshold.

What this means for applicants is that the top 100 is not a single tier. The gap between the 4 institutions above 90 and the 20 in the 80–89 band is substantial. For a student targeting a program with strong employer recognition, the difference between a score of 90+ and one in the low 80s can matter more than the rank number itself, especially since rank ties are possible at the same score.

## Rank Distribution: The Shape of the List

The rank buckets show how quickly the list thins out. There are 10 institutions in the top 10, 20 in the top 20, 30 in the top 30, and 50 in the top 50. The top 100 contains 101 institutions — one more than 100 because of a tie at the boundary. Beyond that, the top 150 holds 150 institutions, the top 200 holds 200, and the top 300 holds 300. The remaining 76 institutions rank 301 or beyond.

A median rank cannot be computed for the 376 institutions: the institution at the median position has only a published rank range of 151-200. Because that median-position institution carries only a rank range, the list cannot be split into a top half and a bottom half by rank. For a student comparing positions, the published buckets are the usable reference: the top 150 holds 150 institutions and the top 200 holds 200. The median also signals that the ranking is not top-heavy — a large number of institutions cluster in the middle and lower ranges.

The count of institutions outside each threshold mirrors this. Outside the top 10, there are 366 institutions; outside the top 20, 356; outside the top 30, 346; outside the top 50, 326; outside the top 100, 275; outside the top 150, 226; outside the top 200, 176; and outside the top 300, 76. These numbers are simply the complement of the bucket counts, but they make the tail visible: 76 institutions sit beyond rank 300, which is a meaningful share of the total list.

## What the Numbers Say About Choosing a Program

For a student selecting a school by subject, the key indicators are the score bands and the country distribution. The top 4 institutions — those scoring 90 or above — are in a league of their own. The next 20, in the 80–89 band, are still elite but measurably different. The 59 institutions in the 70–79 band form a broad middle tier that includes many well-regarded programs, especially in countries like Australia, France, and Canada where national strength in the subject is distributed across multiple universities rather than concentrated in one or two.

The country leaderboard adds context. The US has 69 ranked institutions, which is more than the next two countries combined (UK with 45 and China with 22). But the UK's 45 institutions are concentrated in a smaller geographic area, and many are clustered in the upper ranks. China's 22 institutions reflect a rapid build-out of business education capacity. Australia's 18 and France's 17 show that strong programs exist outside the US–UK axis, which matters for students who want a global experience without paying US or UK tuition levels.

One limitation to keep in mind: the ranking covers only institutions that QS includes in the subject table. Some countries with fewer than 11 ranked institutions — such as those with 1, 2, or 3 entries — do not appear in the top-ten country list, but their programs may still be strong. The ranking is also a snapshot as of the 2026 edition, with data based on the 2026-04-03 release.

## Data Notes

The data in this article comes from the QS World University Rankings by Subject 2026, published by Quacquarelli Symonds (QS), with a data reference date of 2026-04-03. The Accounting & Finance subject table includes 376 institutions globally across 53 countries. The ranking methodology incorporates academic reputation, employer reputation, citations, H-index, and international research network scores. Rankings are determined by parsing the subject-specific table and filtering by country, with rank values taken as the lower integer bound.

The score bands and rank buckets are derived from the published scores and ranks. No median rank is calculated across the 376 institutions, because the institution at the median position has only a published rank range of 151-200. One caveat: the ranking lists only institutions that QS includes in its subject coverage, so some countries may have fewer programs represented than actually exist. Additionally, the top 100 contains 101 institutions due to a tie at the boundary, which slightly affects the bucket counts. No predictions or extrapolations beyond the published data are made in this article.
